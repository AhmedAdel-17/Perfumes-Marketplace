# Brand strategy

**Name status (FACT):** DIYAR / ديار was selected on 1 Sep 2026 and is used in the
codebase; no trademark screening in classes 3 and 35 has been performed and no domain
is registered (`OPEN_DECISIONS.md` D-1). This document treats the name as **provisional**
and writes the strategy so that it survives a rename: everything below is about what the
brand *is*, not what it is called. The one naming-dependent idea — "the houses" (plural of
دار) as the organising metaphor — is noted where it appears and would need a replacement
metaphor if the name changes.

## 1. The central brand problem

A marketplace brand and a hundred house brands must coexist on one screen, and the
founding pack demands both **category authority** for the platform (doc 01 §5) and **brand
independence** for the houses (doc 01 §6). Most marketplaces resolve this by flattening
sellers into SKUs (the thing doc 01 §6 forbids). Most "collective" brands resolve it by
becoming invisible. The strategy has to do neither.

**Resolution (RECOMMENDATION): the platform is the frame, the house is the picture, the
product is the object in it, and trust is the glass.**

| Layer | Owns | Never does |
|---|---|---|
| **Marketplace brand** (the frame) | the standard: verification, information format, honest reviews, one relationship, the visual grammar of the frame (type, space, rhythm) | shout over the house; put its logo on the bottle; sell its own perfume in the category (Phase 3 gate) |
| **House brand** (the picture) | its mark, its story, its photographs, its prices, its voice inside its storefront | override the standard (claims, reviews, fees, support route) |
| **Product brand** (the object) | the name, the bottle, the scent story, the notes | make claims the schema cannot carry |
| **Trust relationship** (the glass) | the reasons a stranger buys: checked, described, reviewed, paid safely, answered | be sold, sponsored or bypassed |

This is the direct answer to "how can the platform build a strong independent identity
while preserving the identity and prestige of each house": **the platform's identity is the
quality of the frame.** A gallery is a brand; it makes the painters more valuable, not less.

## 2. Brand positioning (DOCUMENTED doc 01 §5, restated for brand use)

The specialist, trusted destination for Egyptian local fragrance — the place where the
category is taken seriously. Category authority, not "Amazon for perfumes".

## 3. Brand essence (RECOMMENDATION)

**Considered.** Every house is chosen; every description is checked; every review is
earned; every cost is stated. In Arabic the working word is **مُختار** (chosen/selected),
which also carries "curated" without the imported connotation.

## 4. Brand promise (DOCUMENTED doc 01 §3)

Customers: clear choices, verified sellers, honest reviews, one reliable buying
relationship. Sellers: a professional storefront, order infrastructure, data and
incremental demand without building the technology alone.

## 12. Brand differentiation

| Versus | They are | We are | Evidence to earn it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Generic marketplaces (noon, Jumia, Amazon.eg — FACT, `docs/market/02`) | everything, cheapest, fastest | the category, honestly described, from checked houses | comparison data, reviews per order, refusal rate below market |
| A house's own Instagram | intimate but unverifiable | the same house, plus a guarantee | verified mark, one support route, statements |
| Imported-perfume e-tailers ("original/authentic" positioning — FACT, `docs/market/02`) | authenticity of designer goods | authenticity of *local* makers: who made it, what is in it, how it performs | responsible-party field, bands with source |
| Directories/review communities (Fragrantica lists Egypt — FACT) | opinion without commerce | opinion tied to a delivered order | verified-purchase gate |

## 13. Emotional territory

Discovery with dignity: the pleasure of finding something made here, by someone with a
name, described truthfully, arriving as promised. Pride in local craft without nationalism;
luxury without exclusion (doc 01 §4: value-conscious buyers are the wedge). Emotions to
own: *confidence, curiosity, belonging*. Emotions to avoid: *urgency, envy, fear of
missing out* (doctrine law VII).

## 14. Functional territory

Comparison · verification · honest performance · transparent cost · one relationship ·
reliable delivery · fair economics for makers.

## 15. What the brand should NOT become

- A discount destination ("30–75 % OFF" is the language of the imported-perfume e-tailers
  found in `docs/market/02`; it is not ours).
- A private-label house competing with its sellers.
- A "luxury" brand that excludes the value buyer the model depends on.
- A directory that stops guaranteeing.
- An English-first, Gulf-styled "oud and gold" cliché; Egyptian, contemporary, quiet.
- A platform that lets houses disappear behind it (the SKU trap) or that disappears behind
  houses (the invisible-utility trap).

## 16. Brand risks (RISK)

Name fails screening (D-1) · "verified" claim outruns the checklist · sponsored placement
blurs into ranking · tone drifts to retail as growth pressure rises · the visual frame
becomes a template every house looks the same in (already visible: identical illustrated
bottles, FACT).
