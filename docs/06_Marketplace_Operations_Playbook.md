# 2. Seller onboarding SOP

1.  Qualify: confirm category fit, operational capacity, service area
    and owner identity.

2.  Collect: legal/tax/contact information, bank/payout details, product
    responsibility documents and policies.

3.  Contract: sign seller terms, fees, attribution, fulfilment SLA,
    claims, returns, data and suspension clauses.

4.  Catalogue: import products into standardized fields; reject
    unsupported or confusing claims.

5.  Test: execute a full test order including packaging, confirmation,
    courier, tracking and support.

6.  Train: seller completes order, inventory, cancellation and payout
    training.

7.  Launch: storefront goes live with named owner, escalation contact
    and monitoring window.

8.  Review: 14-day check of confirmation, cancellation, delivery,
    complaints and data quality.

# 4. Listing and claims SOP

-   Use the controlled catalogue schema; no listing publishes with
    missing required fields.

-   Seller owns factual accuracy, but platform moderation checks obvious
    inconsistencies and prohibited claims.

-   No fake designer logos, confusing packaging, false authorization or
    unsubstantiated percentage-similarity claims.

-   Original brand references in search/comparison require an approved
    legal policy.

-   Performance descriptions use defined bands and disclose whether
    provided by seller, platform test or review aggregate.

-   Material product, price, formulation, size or fulfilment changes
    create an auditable revision.

# 6. Multi-seller order policy

-   One customer order may create multiple seller sub-orders.

-   Before checkout, show number of shipments, shipping fees, fulfilment
    estimates and seller-specific restrictions.

-   A failure by one seller must not block fulfilment of other valid
    sub-orders unless the customer requests cancellation and policy
    permits it.

-   Support sees one customer timeline and every seller/courier event.

-   Do not subsidize consolidation until operations and contribution
    economics justify it.

# 7. COD and failed-delivery controls

1.  Validate phone and full address; use confirmation rules based on
    risk, not blanket friction.

2.  Record acquisition source, prior delivery behavior and order anomaly
    flags lawfully.

3.  Confirm high-risk or unusually large orders before seller
    preparation.

4.  Require couriers/sellers to use specific refusal and failure reason
    codes.

5.  Review repeat abuse manually; do not create unlawful or opaque
    automated blacklists.

6.  Allocate failed-delivery cost according to documented responsibility
    and seller agreement.

# 9. Customer support standard

  ----------------------------------------------------------------------------
  **Priority**   **Examples**                          **Target response**
  -------------- ------------------------------------- -----------------------
  P0             Security/privacy breach, unsafe       Immediate internal
                 product pattern, payment integrity    escalation
                 incident                              

  P1             Paid order missing, duplicate charge, Same operational hour
                 serious safety allegation             where staffed

  P2             Late dispatch, wrong item, refund     Same business day
                 delay                                 

  P3             Product question, account help,       Within one business day
                 review moderation

Targets are starting service objectives and must be calibrated to
staffed hours. Every case needs customer, order, seller, category,
status, owner, next action and resolution code.

# 10. Review integrity

-   Only delivered orders receive verified-purchase status.

-   Brands cannot delete or privately suppress negative reviews.

-   Reviews disclose incentives, samples or promotional consideration.

-   Moderation removes abuse, personal data, spam and demonstrably
    unrelated content - not criticism.

-   Ranking separates commercial sponsorship from organic quality
    signals.

-   Suspicious review clusters trigger investigation and an audit
    record.

Enforcement ladder: coaching -\> written warning and cure plan -\>
listing restriction -\> temporary seller suspension -\> termination.
Unsafe, fraudulent or severe legal issues may skip steps.

# 12. Finance and payout reconciliation

-   Reconcile each order to payment/COD record, shipment, discount, fee,
    refund and seller balance.

-   No manual balance adjustment without reason, approver and audit
    evidence.

-   Seller statements separate GMV, shipping, platform fee, payment fee,
    promotion, return and payout.

-   Maintain reserves and payout timing only as permitted and
    contractually disclosed.

-   Daily exception report must reconcile missing or duplicated
    financial events.
