# House map — 51 Egyptian fragrance houses (P0.4.1)

**Checked:** 24 September 2026, desk research only. **Owner of the next step:** the founders
(P0.4.2 interviews draw their sample from here).

**What this is.** The doc 02 §9 week-1 map of 51 houses, each with a segment, its channels,
signals of order activity, product type, how likely it can document its products, a public
contact route, the lead source and the evidence behind it.

**What this is NOT.** Everything below was read from public pages and search results. The
segment tags are a desk hypothesis. They become evidence only when an interview or an
observed order confirms them (doc 02 §3). Follower counts and "customers served" come from
search snippets or the house's own claims on the date above. They move, and houses inflate
them.

## 1. How each column was filled

| Column | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Segment** | `docs/product-strategy/02-personas.md`. **S1** social-first microbrand: sells through its own social channels and, at most, a simple web store; no stores of its own; not in pharmacies or hypermarkets. **S2** online-first or established: its own site **plus** two or more of its own shops, wide marketplace or pharmacy distribution, card or instalment payment, ~100K+ followers, or a corporate parent. **S3** original house: own formulations with a documented perfumer or founder story (extrait, named perfumers, natural perfumery), **whatever the channel** — a secondary S1 means it also sells the S1 way. **S4** traditional shop, kiosk chain or oils merchant. |
| **Type** | `EDP` perfume (eau de parfum / extrait) · `insp` perfume sold as "inspired by" a designer scent · `mist` body splash or mist · `oil` perfume oil · `custom` made to order. Mists are listed because doc 02 §4 names them as the fastest-growing local segment. Whether they are in scope is the **open B-3 boundary**, not decided here. |
| **Activity** | The strongest public proxy found: own shops, marketplace listings, followers, self-reported customers. |
| **Docs** | How likely the house can meet `docs/seller-experience/03` §3, **inferred, not asked**. **H** — sells on noon, Amazon or Jumia, in pharmacies or in its own shops, all of which need commercial registration. **M** — a website with policies and company details, or third-party stockists. **L** — social or DM only, or nothing found. |
| **Contact** | A public **business** channel only (a website or an Instagram handle). No personal phone numbers or personal email addresses were recorded, even where a search result displayed them. |
| **Rank** | Evidence rank per doc 02 §3. **4** — a live storefront was observed (products and prices on its own site or a marketplace). **6** — press mention or social presence only. |
| **Flag** | ⚠C a claims risk under CLAUDE.md §4 (replica or "inspired by" wording that names a designer) · ⚠A activity not confirmed · ⚠F fragrance is a side line. |

## 5. Findings the founders should see now

1. **A specialist marketplace for local perfume exists.** `docs/market/01` §2 says "none
   verified", and that line is restated in the blueprint, the brand positioning and the
   company narrative. **[Perfume Egypt](https://perfume-egypt.com/en)** (IG
   @perfumes.egypt.official) describes itself as a curated multi-brand store of Egyptian
   perfume brands, with more than 1,000 products, nationwide delivery, apparently cash on
   delivery, and a "submit your brand" page for sellers. It also sells alternatives that name
   Creed Aventus and Tom Ford. Its positioning is the opposite of verified makers and
   controlled claims, and it may be the clearest statement of what DIYAR is *not*. It is a
   competitor all the same, and it is also a channel houses already use. It needs a full doc
   02 §5 evidence row with screenshots — **task P0.4.4**.
2. **Source Beauty is the channel local houses already share.** Fifteen of the 51 sell
   through it, alongside international brands, with a "Made in Egypt" section. It is the
   nearest existing answer to "one place for local brands" and needs its own P0.4.4 row.
3. **"Inspired by" is the norm, not the edge.** Five houses flagged ⚠C, including the two with
   mall shops (nspired, Niche Essences). The designer-name question (legal L8, CLAUDE.md §4)
   decides how much of this supply is listable at all. It is not a later detail.
4. **Most local fragrance volume is mists.** Twenty of 51 lead with mists, and most of
   them are skincare brands for which scent is a side line. The B-3 boundary question
   decides whether they are a second wave or out of scope.
5. **The S1 wedge is visible mainly from inside Instagram.** That is the gap above. It is also
   a small piece of product evidence: the houses this plan targets are the hardest to find on
   the open web, which is the discovery problem doc 01 says customers have.

**Not done here, on purpose:** no house was contacted. Outreach is P0.4.2, founder-led, with
the doc 02 §6 script.
