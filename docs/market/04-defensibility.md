# Defensibility

Doc 01 §8: the defensible assets are the normalised catalogue, verified transaction/review
history, seller workflow integration, category reputation and the repeat customer
relationship — not the code. This document tests each against what an incumbent could copy.

| Asset | Why hard to copy | How an incumbent would try | Our answer | Time to become real |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Normalised local catalogue** (schema, vocabularies, notes dictionary, sources) | requires house-by-house work and category expertise; incumbents optimise breadth | add a "local" tag and a notes field | depth per house, sources, moderation, revisions; a notes dictionary in Arabic | months 1–6 |
| **Verified-delivery review graph** | needs our own order stream; cannot be scraped | show unverified reviews with a badge | gate in three layers; masked bylines; no seller deletion | months 3–12 |
| **Seller workflow integration** (queue, SLAs, statements, attribution) | switching cost once a house runs on it; trust in statements | vendor hubs already exist at generic marketplaces | fairness (asymmetric fee, proven), Arabic, simplicity, personal support | months 1–6 |
| **Category reputation** | earned by editorial, verification and consistent honesty | marketing spend | brand principles; no discount voice; visible verification | months 6–18 |
| **Repeat customer relationship** across houses | one phone, one tracking, one support route; cross-house history | incumbents already own accounts | direct return and cross-brand behaviour measured; CRM with consent | months 6–18 |

## Not a moat (say it plainly)

The Next.js codebase · the design system · Paymob/Bosta integrations · the name · being
"first" (unproven and not claimed) · seeded content · any feature a generic marketplace can
ship in a sprint (filters, COD, returns).

## Threats (RISK)

- A generic marketplace launches a "local perfume" storefront with its logistics and
  payment scale. Answer: they will not verify makers or structure scent data at depth; our
  houses' storefronts and fair fees keep them.
- Houses grow and leave for their own websites. Answer: keep the low fee low, keep
  statements honest, keep sending marketplace demand; storefronts remain their acquisition
  page.
- A review-community player adds local commerce. Answer: our reviews are transaction-gated.
- Regulatory change (claims, cosmetics notification, PDPL enforcement) raises costs for
  everyone; a compliant platform becomes the safer channel for houses — an advantage if
  we are compliant first (`docs/legal/`).

## What must be true for defensibility to exist

Attribution operating · ledger honest · reviews gated and growing · catalogue deep in a few
families before wide · brand voice held under growth pressure.
