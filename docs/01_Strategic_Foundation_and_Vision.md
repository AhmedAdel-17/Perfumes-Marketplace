# 1. Business idea

The venture will begin as a multi-vendor website that aggregates
selected Egyptian local perfume brands. Each approved brand receives a
virtual storefront and operating dashboard; customers can discover,
compare and buy products through one trusted platform. A mobile
application, central fulfilment and physical sampling experiences are
later options, not launch requirements.

The initial supply wedge is smaller brands that sell primarily through
shops, Instagram, Facebook or WhatsApp and lack a capable website or
order-management system. The platform then uses proven demand and
transaction data to attract larger online-first brands.

# 4. Initial target segments

## Demand side

-   Digital-first Egyptian fragrance buyers, especially value-conscious
    younger adults already discovering scents through social content.

-   Customers buying affordable local alternatives, original local
    creations or gifts and who want comparison and trusted delivery.

-   Repeat fragrance buyers who value performance, notes, season,
    occasion and price rather than a single brand relationship.

## Supply side

-   First wedge: serious Instagram/WhatsApp sellers without effective
    e-commerce infrastructure.

-   Second wave: established local brands with websites that can be
    offered incremental, platform-attributed demand.

-   Excluded initially: anonymous mixers, counterfeit packaging, sellers
    unwilling to accept service standards, and unverified product
    claims.

# 5. Positioning

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  **Positioning statement:** For Egyptian perfume buyers overwhelmed by
  fragmented options, the platform is the specialist marketplace that
  makes local fragrance easy to compare and safer to buy. Unlike generic
  marketplaces or isolated brand pages, it combines standardized scent
  information, verified reviews and category-specific seller standards.
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------

The phrase \'Amazon for local perfumes\' can explain the model
internally, but it is not a customer proposition or a moat. The business
must win through category authority, trust, data and seller operating
integration.

# 8. Defensibility and growth loop

1.  Curated supply creates credible choice.

2.  Standardized discovery and trust increase conversion.

3.  Orders create verified reviews and preference data.

4.  Data improves recommendations, merchandising and seller insight.

5.  Better demand and tools attract stronger brands.

The defensible assets are the normalized Egyptian scent catalogue,
verified transaction/review history, seller workflow integration,
category reputation and repeat customer relationship. The website code
alone is not defensible.

# 10. Twelve-month definition of progress

-   A verified beachhead of active sellers, not merely signed listings.

-   Completed orders with positive contribution margin and controlled
    cancellation/refusal rates.

-   Evidence that buyers return directly and buy across brands.

-   A repeatable seller onboarding and catalogue-quality process.

-   A documented legal, tax, data and cosmetics compliance position
    reviewed in Egypt.

-   Clear evidence for or against building an app, central fulfilment
    and physical sampling.
